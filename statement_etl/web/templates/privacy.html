Statement → YNAB Import (ph-statement-import) reads the e-wallet or bank statement PDF you upload and creates its transactions in your YNAB budget. It uses your data for that one job, which you start yourself, and for nothing else.

Your statement PDF and its password

  • The PDF is held in the app's memory for your session only. It is never written to disk.
  • The parsed transactions are dropped as soon as they are uploaded to YNAB, or after 30 minutes without activity, whichever comes first.
  • The PDF password only unlocks the file. It isn't a bank or e-wallet login, and it isn't stored: it is used once, to open the PDF.

Your YNAB connection

  • You connect with your YNAB login, on YNAB's own page. The app never asks for your YNAB password or a personal access token.
  • YNAB then gives the app an access token. It is kept in memory only, never in your browser, and dropped when you disconnect or after 30 minutes without activity.
  • You can also remove the app's access at any time in your YNAB account settings.

What is sent where

  • Your transactions go only to YNAB's API, into the plan and account you choose.
  • There are no analytics, no ads, and no other third parties. Your data is never sold or shared.
  • Google Cloud hosts the app as a processor: it runs the app, and doesn't use your data for anything else.

Where it runs, and how it's secured

  • The app runs on Google Cloud in us-west1 (United States). Nothing from a manual upload is stored there.
  • All traffic uses HTTPS. Your browser's cookie only holds a random session id; your statement and YNAB token never go into it.
  • The app's server logs never contain your PDF, its password, your transactions or your YNAB token.

Your rights

You can ask what data the app holds about you, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, or object to how it is used. Because nothing is kept after your session, there is usually nothing left to delete; disconnecting from YNAB, or closing the page for 30 minutes, removes it all.

To ask about or delete your data, email {{ contact_email }}. You can also complain to the Philippines' National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph).

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new "Last updated" date.
