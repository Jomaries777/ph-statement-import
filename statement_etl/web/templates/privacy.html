Statement → YNAB Import (ph-statement-import) reads the e-wallet or bank statement PDF you upload and creates its transactions in your YNAB budget. It uses your data for that one job, which you start yourself, and for nothing else.

Your statement PDF and its password

  • The PDF is held in the app's memory for your session only. It is never written to disk.
  • The parsed transactions are dropped as soon as they are uploaded to YNAB, or after 30 minutes without activity, whichever comes first.
  • The PDF password only unlocks the file. It isn't a bank or e-wallet login. For a manual upload it isn't stored: it is used once, to open the PDF.

Your YNAB connection

  • You connect with your YNAB login, on YNAB's own page. The app never asks for your YNAB password or a personal access token.
  • For a manual upload, YNAB gives the app an access token that is kept in memory only, never in your browser, and dropped when you disconnect or after 30 minutes without activity.
  • You can also remove the app's access at any time in your YNAB account settings.

Optional: Auto-import from Gmail

Auto-import is off unless an invited user turns it on at /auto-import in the hosted app. Visitors who only upload statement PDFs by hand never connect Google and have nothing stored.

  • What emails are read: Auto-import asks for read-only Gmail access (gmail.readonly) and searches only for emails matching each enabled source's statement search (for example, GCash Transaction History emails, or GoTyme Bank Statement of Account emails, with a PDF attachment). Other emails are never opened. Each matching PDF attachment is read in memory only, parsed, imported to YNAB unapproved, and discarded. No email body, PDF or statement transaction is stored — only a run-log entry (message ID, timestamps, source, YNAB account ID, status, and created / skipped counts) so an email is never imported twice.
  • Inbox notifications: So a statement is imported within seconds, Google notifies the app (through Google Cloud Pub/Sub) whenever your inbox changes. A notification holds only your email address and a change number, never an email's content; the app then runs the same statement search. Turning auto-import off stops the notifications.
  • What is stored, and for how long: Because background checks run every 15 minutes without your browser open, turning on auto-import stores one record for your Google account in Google Cloud Firestore (us-west1): your Google account ID and email address, your chosen YNAB plan and e-wallet-to-account mappings, and — encrypted in the app before being written to Firestore — your Gmail refresh token, your YNAB refresh token, and the PDF password inputs you enter once: for GCash, your last name and the last 4 digits of your mobile number; for GoTyme, your date of birth. They are kept until you turn auto-import off.
  • Turn off and delete: Clicking Turn off and delete my data on /auto-import deletes your Firestore record and all of its run-log entries at once, revokes the app's Google token with Google, and signs you out of Google on this app. You can also remove access at any time in your Google Account (Security → Third-party access) and in your YNAB account settings.
  • Google API Services User Data Policy (Limited Use): Statement → YNAB Import's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

What is sent where

  • Your transactions go only to YNAB's API, into the plan and account you choose.
  • Optional: categorize with Gemini. Only if you tick "Categorize new transactions with Gemini" (on the upload page, or for auto-import), the app sends Google's Gemini API your YNAB category and group names and, for each transaction that import created without a category, its date, account name, payee, memo and amount. Not your YNAB token, balances, statement PDF or anything else. On Gemini's free tier, Google may use what is sent to improve its products (Gemini API terms). Leave it unticked and nothing goes to Gemini.
  • There are no analytics, no ads, and no other third parties. Your data is never sold or shared.
  • Google Cloud hosts the app as a processor: it runs the app, and doesn't use your data for anything else.

Where it runs, and how it's secured

  • The app runs on Google Cloud in us-west1 (United States). Nothing from a manual upload is stored there.
  • All traffic uses HTTPS. Your browser's cookie only holds a random session id; your statement and YNAB token never go into it.
  • The app's server logs never contain your PDF, its password, your transactions or your YNAB token.

Your rights

You can ask what data the app holds about you, ask for it to be corrected or deleted, or object to how it is used. For manual uploads nothing is kept after your session: disconnecting from YNAB, or closing the page for 30 minutes, removes it all. If you turned on auto-import, Turn off and delete my data on /auto-import deletes your stored settings and run log immediately.

To ask about or delete your data, email {{ contact_email }}. You can also complain to the Philippines' National Privacy Commission (privacy.gov.ph).

Changes
